Troubleshooting

Something not behaving?

Five faults account for almost every trackpad complaint. Each entry below names the measurement that settles it, the threshold that turns a number into a diagnosis, and the fix that follows — in the order you should try it.

Why does my trackpad pointer stutter or lag?

A report rate below the display refresh, uneven timing, or an acceleration curve fighting your hand. The pointer response check tells you which of the three it is.

Why does my trackpad scrolling jump or feel wrong?

Sometimes a filter quantising your scroll into fixed steps, sometimes just acceleration. The scroll response check reads the shape of the scroll and separates them.

Why do my pinch and swipe gestures not work?

Almost never a hardware fault. Gestures are handled by the operating system before the browser sees them, and the browser reserves some of them for itself.

Why does my trackpad click when I did not ask it to?

A palm landing on the pad while you type, or a click threshold set too low. The tester counts the presses you did not make.

Why has my trackpad stopped working?

Four causes in the order worth checking: a setting, a disabled device, a driver, and then the hardware. The tester says which side of that line you are on.

FAQ

Trackpad test questions

How do I test if my trackpad is working?

Move the pointer around the panel at the top of the page and watch the trail follow it, then tap the panel and scroll over it. The counters tell you whether clicks, drags and scroll events are all arriving. Then run the pointer response check, which samples the trackpad for a second and a half and reports how many position changes per second it actually produces.

Can a browser tell a trackpad apart from a mouse?

Not with certainty. A trackpad and a mouse wheel both produce ordinary wheel events, and the browser cannot see the hardware. It can narrow it down: a pinch gesture arrives as a scroll with the control key held and a small step, a mouse wheel with the control key held arrives with a large even one, a two-finger swipe arrives with a horizontal delta, and a notched wheel repeats the same scroll step over and over while a trackpad does not. The scroll check uses those clues and labels its answer as a best guess rather than a fact.

Why does my scrolling move in jumps?

Run the scroll response check. If it reports a mouse wheel, the steps are fixed notches and the jumps are the wheel itself. If it reports a trackpad and the step variation is high, the scrolling is smooth but uneven, which usually means the operating system is applying an acceleration curve. A very high scroll event rate with large, even steps points at the same thing.

My pinch-to-zoom does not work in the browser. Is the trackpad broken?

Almost certainly not. A pinch usually reaches the browser as a scroll with the control key held, which the browser treats as a zoom command, and some operating system settings swallow it before that happens. The gesture check tells you whether the gesture arrived at all, and whether it was a genuine pinch or a mouse wheel with the control key held, which separates an operating system setting from a hardware fault.

What is a normal report rate for a trackpad?

Most trackpads report between 60 and 120 position changes per second, which matches or exceeds the refresh rate of a typical display. Anything at or above 60 is enough to keep the pointer smooth. A rate below that means the pointer updates less often than the screen redraws, and it will look steppy. The reading is only a true hardware figure in browsers that expose raw sampling; elsewhere it is labelled as an estimate.

Should I use the mouse test or the trackpad test on a laptop?

Use the trackpad test. A trackpad is driven by your finger rather than by a sensor and a wheel, so the mouse test’s precision figure measures something different, and its click tester assumes a physical button that a trackpad may not have. If you have plugged a mouse into the laptop, the mouse test is the right one for that mouse and this one is the right one for the pad.
