About

Hi, I’m Mark.

I run InputScope. It’s a small set of browser-based diagnostics for the two devices you touch every single day — your mouse and your keyboard — built because I got tired of guessing whether a problem was the hardware or my operating system.

Why I made this

Peripherals die quietly. The mouse needs three clicks to open a menu. The keyboard drops one letter every so often and no amount of restarting fixes it. The scroll wheel starts skipping in the middle of a document. In every case the question is the same — is this thing broken, or is it a setting I haven’t found yet? — and there was never a decent answer available, because the tools that might give you one either don’t exist or tell you a number without telling you what it means.

So I made one. InputScope measures what your hardware is actually doing, and then tells you what the number means — including the times the honest answer is “this is fine, there’s a switch in your operating system that you should turn off.” Every result is labelled as an estimate when it is an estimate. I would rather tell you I can’t read your mouse’s true DPI than hand you a confident wrong number.

How it was built

The code was written with AI assistance. I decide what each test measures, what the results mean and what the site says about them; the implementation was drafted with AI help and I review it line by line before it ships. Every measurement gets checked against my own hardware before it goes live.

Worth saying plainly on a site like this one. The whole argument of InputScope is that the measurements are trustworthy — so the person behind them should be visible too. If you would rather talk to a human, that is what I am, and the contact page goes straight to my inbox.

What it costs you

Nothing, and it will stay that way to use. Later on, the informational pages will carry advertising to cover the hosting. Two rules for that: the tools themselves will never have an ad on them, because an advertisement loading in the middle of a measurement shifts the layout and corrupts the readings; and nothing you do inside a test is tracked, logged or sent anywhere. The tests run entirely in your browser and the results never leave the page.

The contact form is the one place your details do travel, because the whole point of it is to get a message to me. The privacy policy says exactly what happens to them.

What is next

The bench is filling up. Headset, gamepad, display and webcam diagnostics are in development — each one gets listed only once there is a real device on the desk to test it against. After that comes the part I think actually matters: a set of written guides for the specific problems people search for when something is behaving strangely. Why a mouse stops double-clicking. Why a keyboard types the wrong letter. Why a scroll wheel starts skipping and what to do about it.

The tools are the useful part, but knowing what to do with the answer is what fixes the problem. That is the point of the site.
