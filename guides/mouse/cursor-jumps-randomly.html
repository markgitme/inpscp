Why does my cursor jump or freeze randomly?

The pointer teleporting across the screen has two classic causes: a dirty sensor throwing it, or a wireless link renegotiating.

Written by Mark · Updated 28 October 2026

The test that settles it

Motion smoothness reports the mean gap between pointer events and the jitter around it — the reading that separates a dirty sensor from a bad radio link. Run the motion test

The pointer teleporting across the screen without you moving the mouse has two classic causes. The first is a dirty or damaged sensor: a speck of dust or a hair under the mouse gets picked up and thrown, so the sensor reports a sudden change in position and the cursor jumps. The second is wireless interference, where the link renegotiates with the receiver and the pointer snaps back to the last known position.

Move the mouse in slow circles and watch for a pattern. Jumps that correlate with fast, jerky movement point at the surface. Jumps that arrive at a steady rate on a clean mousepad point at the radio — move the receiver to a USB port on the same side of the desk as the mouse and see whether it stops.

How to read it

What the motion reading means

Motion smoothness reports the mean gap between pointer events and how much that gap varies. Jitter is where a link problem shows.

The motion readings that separate a dirty sensor from a bad radio link.

ReadingThresholdWhat it means
Mean gap under 12 ms, jitter under 8 ms< 12 ms and < 8 ms“Smooth” — the link is holding steady
Jitter between 8 and 17 ms< 18 ms“Fair” — the link is negotiating occasionally
Jitter of 18 ms or more≥ 18 ms“Choppy” — wireless interference or a low battery
Accuracy high on a pad, low on a desksurface-specificThe sensor is fine. The surface is throwing the pointer
What to try

In order

Clean the sensor

A dry cloth on the lens, and compressed air under the mouse. A single hair under the sensor is enough to throw the pointer.

Change the surface

Test on a mousepad and on a desk. If the jumps only happen on one of them, the surface is the cause.

Move the receiver

Put the receiver in a USB port on the same side of the desk as the mouse, not behind the monitor or in a hub.

Use the cable

If the jumps stop on the wired connection, it is the radio. Charge the battery and check the 2.4 GHz interference from nearby wireless gear.
