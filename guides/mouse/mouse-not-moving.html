Why is my mouse unresponsive or not moving?

Four faults look identical until you test them: asleep, wrong input, driver, or dead. The click tester tells you which.

Written by Mark · Updated 28 October 2026

The test that settles it

Whether the pointer moves and whether the buttons register is the whole diagnosis. The click tester shows both. Run the click tester

“Unresponsive mouse” covers four separate faults, and the difference between them decides what you do next. The mouse is asleep, which happens when a wireless mouse has idled long enough to power down and the first click is consumed waking it up. It is on the wrong input, which happens when a wireless receiver is plugged into another machine or a Bluetooth device is paired to the wrong computer. It is a driver problem, where the operating system has stopped updating the device and a reconnect fixes it. Or it is genuinely dead.

The mouse test above tells you which: if not one button registers but the pointer still moves, the hardware is fine and the problem is upstream in the connection or the driver. If nothing at all happens, the mouse itself has failed.

How to read it

What the click tester means

Two observations separate four faults: does the pointer move, and do the buttons register?

Two observations — movement and button events — split the four causes apart.

ReadingThresholdWhat it means
Pointer moves, no button registers0 button eventsThe hardware is fine. The fault is upstream — the connection, the pairing or the driver
Nothing happens at all0 events, no movementThe mouse has failed. Try it on another port or another machine to confirm
First click does nothing, second worksone lost eventThe mouse was asleep. The first press was consumed waking it
Works on another machine onlymachine-specificWrong input or a driver problem on this machine, not a hardware fault
What to try

In order

Move it and click

If the first press after a pause does nothing, the mouse was asleep. That is normal for a wireless mouse and is not a fault.

Check the input

Wireless receivers are the classic cause. Confirm the receiver is plugged into this machine, and that a Bluetooth mouse is paired to this computer and not a neighbour's.

Reconnect the driver

Unplug and replug, or turn the device off and on. On Windows, Device Manager → Mice and other pointing devices → disable and re-enable the device.

Test elsewhere

Plug into another port, or try the mouse on another machine. If it is dead in both, the mouse is dead.
