Why is my scroll wheel skipping or jittering?

A wheel that skips or moves in uneven steps is almost always a worn scroll encoder — the first part of most mice to fail.

Written by Mark · Updated 28 October 2026

The test that settles it

The scroll encoder test counts notches in each direction and reports the pixel delta per notch — the number that tells a worn encoder from a bad surface. Run the encoder test

A wheel that moves in uneven steps, or jumps two notches at a time, is almost always the scroll encoder wearing out. The encoder is a small wheel with contacts that a spring pushes against as the scroll wheel turns, and dust, dried lubricant and a tired spring all make it skip or double-fire. It is the first part of most mice to fail, usually months before the buttons go.

Cleaning the wheel with a burst of compressed air and a drop of isopropyl alcohol on the axle can rescue a lightly fouled encoder. Beyond that the encoder needs replacing, which in practice means the mouse does. The scroll encoder test in the advanced section counts notches in each direction and reports the pixel delta per notch, which is how you tell a worn encoder from a surface that is simply too slippery.

How to read it

What the encoder test means

The test counts notches in each direction and reports the pixel delta per notch. A healthy wheel is consistent.

The encoder readings, and what each one means.

ReadingThresholdWhat it means
One notch up, one notch down, per detent1 event per detentHealthy encoder
Two events for one physical notch2 events per detentDouble-firing — the encoder is worn or fouled
Some detents produce no event at alldropped notchesThe encoder is skipping. The mouse will get worse, not better
Pixels per notch varies between notchesunstable deltaThe classic early warning. Cleaning usually reverses it
The wheel scrolls sidewayshorizontal eventsYou have a tilt wheel, or something is intercepting the pointer
What to try

In order

Measure the notches

Open the advanced diagnostics and scroll up and down a fixed number of times. Note whether the count matches and whether the pixel delta stays steady.

Blow it out

Compressed air under the wheel while it spins. Most encoder faults are dust, and this fixes a surprising share of them.

Clean the axle

A drop of isopropyl alcohol on the wheel axle, then spin it by hand to work it in.

Decide

If the delta still varies, the encoder is worn through. That is a replacement, and in practice that means a new mouse.
